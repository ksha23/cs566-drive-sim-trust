# Results — full run (Sep 27, 2026; §11 DL3DV and §14–16 added Sep 28–29; §8 closed loop with fixers, §15 eight test roads and §18 final consolidated results updated Sep 30; final round on all eight roads Nov 24, §19)

Every number below is read from a file in `results/` (named next to each table); every figure is regenerated by
`scripts/09_summarize_figures.sh` (Para-Lane) or `scripts/11_hugsim_nta.sh` / `scripts/13_hugsim_reproduction.sh`
(HUGSIM). The one-night prototype (v1, Sep 26) is kept in `v1_prototype/`; **its claims are superseded** by this run
(different split, more data, fixed on-path damage) — see the last section.

Naming follows the proposal: [1] Difix3D+ (the method we reimplement), [2] FaithFusion, [3] HUGSIM, [4] NVIDIA Fixer,
[5] ReconDreamer (NTA/NTL-IoU only); "our fixer" = the smaller version trained from scratch (15.2M-param U-Net).

"Difix-FT" rows are a fine-tune of the **released** Difix weights; our from-scratch (SD-Turbo) reimplementation of the
Difix training recipe is §11 ("our Difix-ref reimpl.").

## 1. Setup
* Para-Lane 0315, front camera 960x540. Split by scene: **train** scene_20–24, **val** scene_19 (checkpoint and config
  selection only), **test** scene_16 and scene_17. Lanes are ~3 m and ~6–7.7 m apart (scene_17 is the widest).
* Evaluation: for each ordered lane pair A→B of a test scene, lane A's splat is rendered at lane B's real poses
  (every 2nd frame) and compared with B's **real** image; plus A's held-out on-path frames (0 m). 101 / 513 / 256 images
  in the 0 m / ~3 m (1–4.5 m) / ~6 m (>4.5 m) bins. CIs: 95% bootstrap over lane-pair sequences.

## 2. Street splats (milestone 1) — `onpath/ONPATH.md`
On-path held-out quality per lane: PSNR 21.2–25.9 dB, LPIPS 0.25–0.45 (24 lanes, 8 scenes). Cycle-reconstruction
splats (trained only on ±3 m renders) are 1–1.5 dB / 0.1–0.2 LPIPS worse on-path, as intended.

## 3. Training pairs (milestone 2) — `pairs/pair_counts.json`

on-path identity 1,600. Val 744, test 1,601 (scene_16 613, scene_17 1,026) eval pairs.

Findings:
* **Trust decays steeply with offset**, and not only in image metrics: CLRNet lane agreement with the real frame falls
  from 0.98 (on-path) to 0.07 at ~3 m and 0.02 at ~6 m for the raw splat; paint recall to 0.15 at ~6 m.
* **Zero-shot Difix [1] makes images look better (LPIPS) but not more faithful**: its paint precision and F1 are no
  better than the raw render (on-path it drops to 0.78 precision — invented/redrawn markings), though it recovers some
  CLRNet lanes.
* **Fine-tuning Difix on our driving pairs changes this**: Difix-FT is the best method on every off-path faithfulness
  metric (paint P/R/F1, NTL-IoU, lane F1) and LPIPS, with a moderate fake-vehicle rate.
* **NVIDIA Fixer [4], driving-trained, is the most conservative strong fixer zero-shot**: best off-path PSNR/SSIM at
  ~3 m, only −0.9 dB on-path, few fake vehicles, and it recovers many lanes (NTL-IoU 0.07 → 0.41 at ~3 m) — but
  in-domain fine-tuning (Difix-FT) recovers more lanes and texture.
* **Our small from-scratch fixer does not transfer**: all five runs are competitive on scene_16 (e.g. base_s0: on-path
  24.9 dB, ~3 m LPIPS 0.257; `eval/tables.md`) but damage scene_17, so across both test scenes they are below the raw
  render on-path and recover almost no lanes off-path (CLRNet lane F1 ≤ 0.12 at ~3 m). The GAN term raises paint
  precision on-path (0.97) but also the fake-vehicle rate at ~6 m. Seeds differ a lot (on-path PSNR 18.8–22.3 dB across
  runs), so the small model is also unstable. Verdict: it does not invent lanes, but it removes true structure rather
  than restoring it, and does not generalise across scenes with 5 training scenes.
* **Difix-ref fairness** (`eval/tables.md`): with the reference ≥ 2 s away instead of the nearest source-lane frame,
  Difix-ref loses most of its on-path advantage (LPIPS 0.140 → 0.192) and some off-path (0.340 → 0.363 at ~3 m). Its
  near-reference on-path numbers are therefore optimistic (the reference is 0.1 s from the target).

On scene_16, distilling Difix-FT's restorations into the splat reproduces Difix3D+'s central claim — the 3D model
itself becomes more faithful off-path (lane F1 0.18 → 0.90 at ~3 m), while on-path quality is kept (25.3 vs 25.6 dB).
On scene_17 the gain is much smaller (both-scene means in `eval/tables.md`: lane F1 at ~3 m 0.07 → 0.28), and our
small fixer brings no lane gain. The restorer matters most: our Difix-ref reimpl. ≈ Difix-FT > NVIDIA Fixer ≈
Difix-ref > ours. **Across eight test roads (§15) the progressive gain is strongly road-dependent**: +0.52 to +0.73 lane F1
at ~3 m on scene_16, scene_3 and scene_4, +0.06 to +0.31 on the other five. A schedule
closer to Difix3D+ Alg. 1 (four 1.5 m steps, 1.5k iterations each; the paper gives no step size) gives similar LPIPS
but slightly lower lane agreement than our two coarse steps on scene_16. On-path quality is preserved by all arms
(25.3–25.4 dB vs 25.6 raw). Fly-through: `figures/flythrough_raw_vs_progressive_difixft.mp4`.

**FaithFusion [2]** (`faithfusion/`, `../docs/faithfusion.md`): its EIG map (their rasterizer, their loss-weight
schedule) weighting the pseudo-view loss makes **no measurable difference** vs. a flat weight with the same restorer
(paired Δ at ~3 m: LPIPS +0.005 [0.005, 0.006], NTL-IoU −0.016 [−0.036, 0.001]). Note their repo only enables EIG
weighting with an unreleased restorer. **Does the map predict hallucinations?** Partly: for invented lane paint
(fixer paint with no real paint within ~10 px) EIG reaches AUROC 0.69 (Difix), 0.76 (Difix-FT), 0.67 (ours) at ~3 m,
better than a reprojection-consistency map (0.60–0.64), but near chance at ~6 m (0.44–0.59) and for Difix-ref. For
"changed and wrong" pixels, plain splat coverage (1 − alpha) predicts best (0.61–0.77) (`faithfusion/halluc_auroc.md`).
These are scene_16 numbers; over all eight roads (§19) the EIG weighting slightly lowers lane F1 at ~3 m, and the
map's AUROC for invented paint at ~3 m is 0.67–0.75 across five fixers.

## 7. Road-surface prior (stretch, ViSE-style) — scene_16, `eval/tables.md`
A height+slope road field fitted to LiDAR, with road Gaussians pulled onto it and flattened, raises lane agreement
**without any fixer**: NTL-IoU 0.18 → 0.41 and lane F1 0.18 → 0.52 at ~3 m, paint F1 0.47 → 0.61, LPIPS 0.493 → 0.463,
on-path unchanged (25.6 dB). A geometric prior is a cheap, hallucination-free complement to fixers. On all eight test
roads (§15) it helps every time, but less than on scene_16 (lane F1 +0.08 to +0.35 at ~3 m, ≤ +0.08 at ~6 m).

  are exercised at ~3 m and ~6 m, on nuScenes, where the Para-Lane-trained fixers transfer poorly (NTA-IoU above).

## 10. What reproduces from the main papers, what does not
* **Difix3D+ [1]**: *reproduces* — a fixer fine-tuned on in-domain pairs plus progressive distillation makes the 3D
  model more faithful off-path (scene_16), and the released model lowers LPIPS zero-shot. *Does not reproduce as a
  general claim* — zero-shot, the released model improves perceptual metrics without improving faithfulness (paint
  precision/F1; over the 8 test roads of §15 it does raise CLRNet lane F1 at ~3 m, +0.38, but paint precision only
  0.50 → 0.53). The progressive gain is scene-dependent: over eight test roads it is large on three of them
  (+0.73 lane F1 at ~3 m on scene_16, +0.52 to +0.56 on scene_3 and scene_4, +0.06 to +0.31 on the other five; §15). Per-frame fixers trained on driving pairs help consistently
  (+0.30 to +0.80 on all eight).
* **HUGSIM [3]**: LTF scores reproduce in trend and, for hard/extreme, in value; fixers do not measurably change policy
  scores (Difix, Difix-FT; 75 paired runs each) or hurt them (NVIDIA Fixer −0.05, our U-Net −0.21).
* **ReconDreamer [5]** metrics: detector agreement decays with offset (NTA-IoU −16% at 3 m); lane agreement (NTL-IoU)
  decays much faster on Para-Lane.
* **FaithFusion [2]**: EIG maps are computable with their code and partially predict invented lane paint near the
  path, but EIG loss weighting does not change the refined splat measurably in our setting.
* **NVIDIA Fixer [4]**: driving-trained restoration transfers well across datasets (best NTA-IoU on nuScenes, good
  zero-shot lane recovery on Para-Lane, best PSNR on EUVS and MV2), but on MV2 it invents the most vehicles (§14);
  fine-tuning it fits on 24 GB.
* **Difix reimplementation**: our trainer written from the paper beats the official trainer at the same data and budget,
  on our driving data *and* on DL3DV (the paper's data; §11). Our ref-view model trained on 7 DL3DV scenes ties
  NVIDIA's released Difix-ref on held-out DL3DV LPIPS, but not on FID. The paper's DL3DV-Benchmark numbers remain
  unchecked (repo not authorized).
* **Beyond the papers, new in §14–15**: real cross-vehicle ground truth (MV2, India) puts a one-vehicle-over render at
  the Para-Lane ~6 m level of trust; lateral separation, not the ~0.4 m camera-height difference, drives it there.
  Para-Lane-trained fixers transfer only partially (as on EUVS), and the released generic Difix-ref helps most. On
  all eight Para-Lane test roads, Difix-FT, the progressive update and the road prior each raise lane agreement
  without adding fake vehicles (Difix-FT by far the most). Our Difix-ref reimplementation matches Difix-FT on lanes
  but invents vehicles on every road.

## 11. Our reimplementation of Difix training [1] — `difix_reimpl/`, `../docs/IMPLEMENTATION_NOTES_difix.md`, `../docs/difix_reimpl_validation.md`
Our own trainer (`src/fixers/difix_reimpl/`): SD-Turbo initialization (not the released weights), single step at
τ = 200, reference-view mixing (self-attention over views, 2D weights inherited), frozen VAE encoder + rank-4 LoRA
decoder with encoder→decoder skip convs, full U-Net fine-tune, L2 + LPIPS + 0.5·Gram; fp32 master weights + 8-bit AdamW.
Same architecture as the released model; training loop, data pipeline and losses are ours.

**Validation vs. the official trainer and released weights, on DL3DV** (the paper's data; `dl3dv/summary.md`,
`dl3dv/paired.csv`, details and caveats in the validation doc §1). Access was granted for DL3DV-10K-Sample only
(11 scenes; Benchmark and ALL-960P still HTTP 403, `dl3dv/access_check.json`). Pairs were built the paper's way for
DL3DV: sparse reconstruction (splat on every 30th or 60th frame, rendered at the others) + underfitting (25 % of the
schedule); 7 train / 1 val / 3 test scenes, 9,248 training pairs. All runs from SD-Turbo, 1,801 steps, same pairs:

* **Heading is not what breaks a street splat — position is.** Turning the camera up to ±15° about its own center
  costs every arm ≤ 0.35 dB against an exact ground truth on the same content, lane agreement stays ≥ 0.91, and CLRNet

  more than at 0°, ≤ 0.10). On the lateral + yaw poses they are ≤ 0.19 per image, at levels like §5 (Difix-ref / our
  reimpl. 0.14–0.15). The ranking of §5 is unchanged: Difix-FT /
  our reimpl. are the only arms whose lanes are both right and stable off-path.

  its protocol on MARS.

## 17. Our own renderer (hybrid) — `own_renderer/`, `../docs/own_renderer.md`
`src/splats/own_render.py` is a drop-in replacement for `splats.gs.render`. **Ours** (PyTorch, differentiable by
autograd): world→camera transform, 3D covariance from wxyz quaternion + log-scales, EWA projection with the
perspective Jacobian, gsplat's low-pass dilation (eps2d = 0.3, "classic" mode), conics, 3σ radii and all culling,
degree-0–3 SH → RGB, expected depth, sky environment map, and the `info` that DefaultStrategy densification reads.
**gsplat's** (CUDA): tile intersection/sorting (`isect_tiles`) and per-pixel alpha compositing (`rasterize_to_pixels`)
with its backward. `src/splats/torch_ref_render.py` replaces those two kernels with plain PyTorch (small crops only).
Training with it: `fit_splat.py --renderer own` (default stays gsplat; nothing above changes).

**Equivalence on the existing splats** (scene_16 and scene_17, lane clip_0; 24 frames at 0 m / ~3 m / ~6 m;
`own_renderer/check.json`, `summary.json`):

| | own vs gsplat |
|---|---|
| RGB mean / max abs difference | 1.1e-6 / 0.076 (single pixels; 0.02–0.17% of pixels differ by one 8-bit level) |
| PSNR between the two renders, mean (worst frame) | 90.8 dB (76.4 dB) |
| alpha mean / max; expected depth mean / max (α > 0.5) | 1.2e-6 / 0.004; 7e-6 m / 0.18 m |
| gradient cosine of an L1 photometric loss: means / scales / quats / opacities / SH / sky / camera pose | ≥ 0.99996 / 0.999998 / 0.999999 / 0.999997 / 0.999997 / 1.0 / 0.9999996 |
| densification statistic (screen-space mean gradient): cosine; grow decision agreement | ≥ 0.99996; ≥ 99.9995% of Gaussians |
| forward / forward+backward per 960×540 frame (mean) | 8.6 vs 5.4 ms (1.5× slower) / 28.9 vs 15.9 ms (1.7× slower) |
| peak extra memory, fwd+bwd, mean (max) | 626 (1,362) vs 633 (700) MiB |

On 64×64 crops the pure-PyTorch reference agrees with our hybrid to 8e-7 (RGB) and gradient cosine ≥ 0.9999965, and
with gsplat to 6e-4 / ≥ 0.9999998. A first version using `torch.matmul` on 3×3 blocks was 12× slower than gsplat
per training step; broadcast multiply-and-sum brought it to 1.7×.

## Superseded prototype claims (v1, `v1_prototype/`)
v1 (one test scene, 2 training scenes, 21-min U-Net, no identity pairs) claimed that our small U-Net "beats zero-shot
Difix on every off-path metric" and "matches Difix-ref on paint precision". With proper held-out scenes and CLRNet lane
agreement this does **not** hold: the U-Net's high paint precision came with low recall (erased paint), and it does
not transfer to scene_17. v1's on-path damage (25.6 → 22.6 dB on scene_16) was fixed by identity pairs (24.9 dB).
